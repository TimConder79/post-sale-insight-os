# Post-Sale Operating System — Product Reference

This is a concise implementation reference, not a replacement for the full book.

## Core thesis
- Retention is a designed outcome.
- The Post-Sale is the Growth Pipeline.
- Before the sale, progression is designed. After the sale, we mostly hope for it.

## Customer system
**Customer Journey → Inflection Points → Expected Customer Progression → Plays → Evidence → Customer Health**

- Journey: broader experience from purchase toward intended outcomes.
- Progression: meaningful change from the reason purchased toward intended outcomes.
- Play: what the company does to support progression.
- Evidence: how we know whether meaningful change occurred.
- Health: interpretation of accumulated evidence about whether the customer is progressing toward and able to sustain the outcomes that justified the relationship.

Canonical distinction: **Activity tells you what happened. Progression tells you what changed. Evidence tells you whether that change actually occurred.**

## Revenue system
**Existing-Customer Revenue Visibility → Revenue Opportunity → Evidence → Exit Criteria → Pipeline Stage → Probability → Timing → Forecast**

The pipeline contains revenue opportunities, not customers. Every customer creates at least one retention opportunity at purchase. Expansion opportunities enter when they become specific/manageable.

Stages: **Identify 20% → Align 40% → Advocate 60% → Intent 90% → Net Revenue Close 100%**.

Intent means explicit commitment/verbal yes plus a credible path, not contractual close.

Pipeline stages advance when observable evidence satisfies exit criteria, not because a play was completed, time elapsed, usage increased, or a CSM believes the customer is healthy.

## Important distinctions
- Health ≠ Pipeline.
- Capacity ≠ forecast input.
- Customer Journey ≠ Customer Progression.
- Plays support progression; play completion is not evidence that progression occurred.
- Pipeline makes revenue visible and shows where specific retention/growth opportunities stand; Journey/Progression/Evidence make customer progression observable.

## Capacity
**Customer Journey / Inflection Points → Service Moments → Designed Effort → Customer Demand → Resource Capacity → Staffing**

ARR tells the value of customers. It does not tell the work required to serve them. 65% direct availability is a planning starting point, not a universal benchmark.
